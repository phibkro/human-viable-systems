# 01 — Means–ends inversion: AI, metrics, and organizational purpose

## Core idea

Technology is a means to an end. An organization can invert that relationship by optimizing for the adoption or measured output of a tool rather than for the human or customer value it was intended to produce.

The Proton investigation discussed in our conversation provides a motivating case: reporting described leadership pressure around AI adoption, remarks dismissive of security-related delay, and employee concerns about dissent. **These are allegations and reported statements, not proof that Proton's products are insecure.** Article: https://www.404media.co/manifestos-fear-and-loser-thinking-inside-protons-ai-reckoning/

## Mechanism

1. The organization has a legitimate objective: deliver reliable, useful software.
2. It introduces an instrument: AI-assisted development.
3. It adopts proxies: percentage of AI-written code, velocity, release frequency.
4. Rewards and pressure become attached to the proxies.
5. People adapt to the proxies, potentially neglecting verification, learning, maintenance, or user value.
6. Dissent becomes costly if the proxy is treated as an ideological commitment.

This is related to **Goodhart's law**, **Campbell's law**, **goal displacement**, **instrumental rationality**, and **principal–agent problems**. None implies that AI itself is undesirable.

## Diagnostic questions

- What end is the tool meant to serve, and who benefits?
- Which metrics are proxies rather than outcomes?
- What important properties are unmeasured?
- Can practitioners challenge the policy or halt unsafe work?
- Does increased output coincide with increased maintainability, learning, reliability, and customer satisfaction?

## Corrective design

Measure outcomes alongside throughput; give responsible workers decision rights and protected channels for criticism; budget for verification and maintenance; evaluate AI use as one possible method, not an obligation in itself.

## HVSM connection

The inversion is visible as a misalignment between **purpose**, **agency**, and **viability**. A policy may satisfy a throughput target while degrading the human capabilities that enable future delivery.
