# Human Viable Systems — research and framework notes

**Status:** working conceptual synthesis, not a validated scientific theory.  
**Purpose:** separate the motivating philosophy from a practical, participatory organizational diagnostic method.

## Thesis

An organization should be assessed not only by whether it achieves its stated goals, but by whether its activities sustain and develop the **agency, capabilities, relationships, and viability** of the humans and other systems it depends on and affects. The viability of a whole does not guarantee the viability of its parts.

## Keep the framework lean

**Core diagnostic:** purpose, agency, viability.  
**Supporting lenses:** operations, coordination, governance, ecology, behavioral design.  
**Research background:** VSM, 4EM, sociotechnical systems design, soft systems methodology, Ostrom, ecological economics, autopoiesis, self-determination theory.

A practitioner should be able to use the three core views without reading the entire theoretical literature. Every theoretical addition must earn its place by improving a diagnosis, an intervention, or an evaluation.

## Recursive scope

Person → team → organization → network of organizations → institution/region → national and transnational systems. These are **analytical levels**, not claims that a country or a corporation is literally a biological organism. At every level, ask what the unit requires to reproduce its capacities, which other units it depends on, and whose autonomy it constrains.

## Epistemic distinctions

- **Reported fact:** evidence about a particular case, subject to verification.
- **Interpretation:** a causal or ethical reading of that evidence.
- **Model:** a simplified conceptual tool for discussion and diagnosis.
- **Hypothesis:** a relationship to test rather than assume (e.g., greater voice improves long-run resilience).
- **Normative commitment:** the decision to treat human agency and welfare as ends, not merely inputs to production.

## Suggested first pilot

Choose one team or transformation. Conduct participatory interviews, map its stated purpose, decisions and authority, work dependencies, and signs of capacity regeneration/depletion. Produce a tension map and 2–3 jointly designed interventions. Reassess outcomes after an agreed period. The test is whether HVSM reveals actionable tensions that ordinary goal/process mapping overlooks.
