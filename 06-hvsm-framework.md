# 06 — Human Viable Systems Modeling (HVSM)

**Working definition:** A participatory enterprise analysis and organizational design framework for diagnosing whether a system's purposes, authority structures, and operations sustain the agency and viability of its constituent and affected agents over time.

## Intended role

HVSM is a **practical conceptual framework**, closer in ambition to 4EM than to a predictive scientific theory. It should be usable in workshops and organizational reviews. Its underlying philosophy can be broad; its working vocabulary must remain small.

## Central commitment

Treat people as agents with their own ends, not merely inputs to an organization's optimization problem. Treat teams and organizations as potentially self-organizing operational units, while **not** equating collective operational agency with human moral standing.

## Recursive modeling

The focal unit can shift: person, team, organization, interorganizational network, institution, region, nation, or international system. At each scale, map:

1. The unit's purpose and beneficiaries.
2. Its members and dependencies.
3. The decisions it can make and contest.
4. The capabilities and resources it must maintain.
5. The exchanges that sustain or deplete it.
6. The feedback and governance that allow adaptation.

The recursion is an **analytical technique**, not an assertion that all levels have identical interests or rights. Conflicts between levels must be modeled rather than wished away.

## Three core views

### Purpose view

**Entities:** purposes, beneficiaries, goals, outcomes, measures, constraints.  
**Questions:** What is the work for? Who defines success? Which measures are proxies? Whose interests are missing?

### Agency view

**Entities:** agents, collectives, roles, decisions, authority, accountability, dependencies, contestation rights.  
**Questions:** Who can act, refuse, object, learn, and change the rules? Are responsibility and authority aligned?

### Viability view

**Entities:** capabilities, resources, regenerative processes, depletion, risks, thresholds, externalities.  
**Questions:** Which capacities are being built or consumed? Who bears the costs? What fails under stress?

## Supporting views when needed

- **Operations:** processes, work, tools, and resources.
- **Coordination:** inter-team dependencies and feedback.
- **Governance:** rule-making, representation, conflict resolution.
- **Ecology:** material, social, institutional, and environmental dependencies.

Do not require every view for every analysis.

## Minimal conceptual metamodel

`Agent`, `Collective`, `Purpose`, `Outcome`, `Activity`, `Capability`, `Resource`, `Decision`, `Authority`, `Dependency`, `Constraint`, `Feedback`.

Suggested relations: `pursues`, `benefits`, `contributesTo`, `requires`, `authorizes`, `canContest`, `dependsOn`, `regenerates`, `depletes`, `affects`, `evaluates`.

An essential cross-view path:

**Purpose → Policy → Activity → Agent/Capability → Viability effect → Feedback → Policy revision**

## Diagnostic patterns

| Pattern | Symptom | Potential intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Means–ends inversion | Tool adoption replaces outcome quality | Re-anchor measures in beneficiary outcomes |
| Authority–responsibility gap | Teams own results but cannot control decisions | Delegate protected decision rights |
| Capability depletion | Output rises as skills, trust, or maintenance erode | Budget learning, repair, and regeneration |
| Suppressed feedback | Bad news cannot travel upward | Protected escalation and contestation |
| Externalized harm | Benefits accrue to one unit; costs fall on another | Include affected agents in governance and evaluation |
| Coordination failure | Autonomous teams cannot resolve dependencies | Minimal shared rules and conflict-resolution channels |

## Participatory assessment process

1. **Scope:** select the focal unit, timescale, and affected agents.
2. **Elicit:** collect stated goals and lived accounts from different roles, including dissenting views.
3. **Model:** build purpose, agency, and viability maps; trace key policies across views.
4. **Diagnose:** identify contradictions, unprotected dependencies, and depleted capacities.
5. **Redesign:** co-design a small number of changes to authority, processes, measures, or resources.
6. **Review:** track both intended benefits and unintended effects; revise the model.

**Deliverables:** a purpose map, decision-rights map, capacity/viability map, tension register, intervention plan, and review criteria.

## Relation to established methods

- **4EM:** use its goal, business rule, process, actor/resource, and concept models for organizational traceability. HVSM adds explicit agency and viability diagnostics.
- **VSM (Stafford Beer):** use recursive autonomy, coordination, and adaptation. HVSM makes human welfare, protected agency, and affected stakeholders explicit.
- **Sociotechnical systems design:** borrow joint optimization and minimal critical specification.
- **Soft Systems Methodology:** borrow multiple worldviews and problem structuring when goals are contested.
- **ArchiMate:** optional notation for links to strategy, capabilities, processes, and technology; not the ethical foundation.
- **Ostrom / commons governance:** borrow participation, accountable rule-making, conflict resolution, and nested institutions.

## Adoption case

The argument is not that HVSM always maximizes profit. It can help organizations expose hidden costs of apparent productivity: lost expertise, burnout, brittle coordination, customer distrust, and inability to adapt. These are **hypotheses to assess in context**, not guaranteed returns. Mission-driven institutions, cooperatives, public services, and organizations undertaking complex transformation may have particularly clear reasons to use it.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate organizational survival with ethical goodness.
- Do not assume all stakeholder interests can be harmonized.
- Do not let aggregate organizational health conceal harm to individuals.
- Do not confuse self-organization with the absence of accountability.
- Do not claim literal biological autopoiesis for every social system.
- Do not turn qualitative human flourishing into a simplistic numerical KPI.
- Do not add a new theoretical concept unless it changes a modeling question, diagnosis, or intervention.

## Minimum viable pilot

Take one real team or organizational change. Map one stated goal, three consequential decisions, the actors affected, and three capacities required for future operation. Interview people across authority levels. Identify one mismatch between stated purpose and lived operation. Co-design a change and establish review criteria. Compare whether HVSM surfaced something missed by ordinary process/goal modeling.

## Open research questions

- What constitutes a viable unit at each scale?
- What rights belong to humans, versus what operational freedoms belong to collectives?
- How are conflicting viability constraints negotiated?
- How can organizational quality be assessed without making it another gameable metric?
- What institutional mechanisms prevent higher-level units from preserving themselves by depleting lower-level units?
