# 04 — Organizational hierarchy, worker agency, and self-governance

## Core idea

An organization can achieve financial or operational goals while its workers lose autonomy, voice, skills, and identification with their work. Centralization is not always wrong, but it becomes hazardous when those responsible for outcomes cannot challenge decisions or shape the conditions under which they work.

## Key distinctions

- **Delegated autonomy:** management permits discretion, potentially revocable at will.
- **Protected autonomy:** decision rights and appeal mechanisms are institutionalized.
- **Coordination:** shared rules needed to make interdependent work coherent.
- **Domination:** asymmetric power without meaningful accountability or recourse.
- **Operational agency:** capacity to act and adapt; not the same as human moral standing.

## Existing intellectual roots

- Stafford Beer's **Viable System Model**: recursive viable units, operational autonomy, coordination, adaptation.
- **Sociotechnical systems design**: joint design of work and technology, autonomous work groups, minimal critical specification.
- **Elinor Ostrom**: participation in rules, monitoring, conflict resolution, nested governance.
- **Self-determination theory**: autonomy, competence, relatedness.
- **Marxian alienation** and **labor process theory**: control over work and the ends of production.
- **Organizational justice** and **psychological safety**: voice, fairness, and interpersonal risk.

## Diagnostic questions

Who sets goals? Who does the work? Who has the authority to stop it? Who bears the costs of failure? Can affected workers revise the rules? Are expertise and dissent treated as organizational resources or obstacles?

## Corrective patterns

Local decision rights with explicit boundaries; participatory goal-setting; transparent escalation; independent safety/quality authority; conflict resolution; protected worker voice; shared learning; governance proportional to dependencies.

## Caveat

Flatness is not automatically democratic, and decentralization can conceal informal power. Evaluate actual authority, accountability, and outcomes rather than organizational charts alone.
