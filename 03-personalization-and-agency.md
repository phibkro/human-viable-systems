# 03 — Personalization, privacy, and authority over personal data

## Core idea

Personalization creates a tension: a system may understand a person well enough to be genuinely useful while the provider's incentives may favor retention, behavioral steering, or data monetization. Awareness of personalization does not make its benefits disappear.

## Distinct dimensions

- **Privacy:** who learns what, and how widely does information spread?
- **Control:** who authorizes collection, retention, inference, and onward sharing?
- **Alignment:** whose purposes are optimized?
- **Portability:** can the user leave without losing their accumulated context?
- **Contestability:** can a person inspect, correct, limit, or challenge consequential inferences?

Data ownership is not identical to data protection. Treating personal data as saleable property could empower people in some transactions but also make rights easier to surrender under economic pressure. Limited, revocable permissions may better preserve agency than permanent alienation.

## Design possibility

A user-controlled personal context layer can provide purpose-scoped information to applications. A writing tool might receive style preferences without receiving unrelated health, location, or relationship history. Such an architecture still requires strong enforcement, auditability, and institutional protections; a consent screen alone does not guarantee meaningful control.

## Relevant connections

Privacy engineering, capability-based security, data fiduciaries, informational self-determination, contextual integrity (Helen Nissenbaum), human-centered AI, platform governance, personal data stores.

## HVSM connection

Personal data is both an input to useful service and a potential means of controlling its subject. Model the person as an agent with continuing authority over the purposes and conditions of use.
