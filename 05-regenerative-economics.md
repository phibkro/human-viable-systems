# 05 — Regenerative economics and the sustainability of constituent systems

## Core idea

An organization requires exchanges with its environment, but must also maintain the internal capacities that make those exchanges possible. Revenue and growth are not the same as resilience, quality, or regeneration.

A firm can increase profits while depleting worker knowledge, trust, maintenance capacity, customer welfare, or environmental resources. The same issue recurs at larger scales: organizational networks, regions, countries, and international systems.

## Conceptual vocabulary

- **Growth:** increase in measured economic activity.
- **Development:** improvement in qualitative capabilities and outcomes.
- **Circularity:** reuse and recirculation of materials and resources.
- **Regeneration:** restoration or strengthening of capacities required for future activity.
- **Viability:** ability to continue adapting and functioning under changing conditions.
- **Throughput:** physical and energetic flows across an economic system.

Circularity alone does not guarantee humane governance. A highly efficient recycling enterprise can still exploit workers. Likewise, economic growth can coincide with genuine improvements; the critique concerns growth treated as an unconditional end.

## Autopoiesis analogy

Living systems maintain their organization through ongoing exchange and self-production. Organizations likewise reproduce knowledge, roles, trust, routines, and institutions. **This is an analogy and analytical lens, not a claim that organizations literally meet the biological definition of autopoiesis.** Virus-versus-organism comparisons can illustrate dependence and reciprocity, but should not be treated as a moral taxonomy of biological entities.

## Viability accounting

For each relevant capacity ask: What replenishes it? What depletes it? Who bears the cost? How quickly can it recover? Which thresholds should never be crossed?

An illustrative condition is **capacity regeneration ≥ capacity depletion over a relevant horizon**, but capabilities are heterogeneous and cannot simply be reduced to a single number.

## Intellectual roots

Herman Daly's steady-state economics; Kate Raworth's doughnut economics; ecological economics; regenerative economics; resilience theory; commons governance; industrial ecology; systems ecology.

## HVSM connection

An organization's viability is constrained by its relationships with the people, communities, institutions, and ecosystems on which it depends. A viable whole may contain nonviable or exploited parts; the framework must reveal this rather than average it away.
